# Model BSIM4 (Level 14 / Version 4.0 MOSFET): 4-Bit CMOS Inverter Bank Hamming Distance Switching Activity, Biot-Savart Magnetic Field FFT & Spectrogram Analysis

## 1. Executive Summary

This technical report investigates the transient current dynamics, directional switching asymmetry, and electromagnetic (EM) side-channel emission profile of a **4-bit parallel CMOS inverter bank** based on the nanometer-scale **BSIM4 (Level 14 / Version 4.0) MOSFET model** from `Hamming.txt` and the circuit netlist `Hamming.cir` in `Model BSIM4 Hamming Distance Config and Data`.

Three primary domains are characterized:
1. **Time-Domain Switching Activity**: Correlating 4-bit bus transitions ($0000 \to 0101 \to 0111 \to 0011 \to 1111 \to 0000$), Hamming Distance ($HD$), and output voltages ($Y_0..Y_3$) with the overall supply current $I_{DD} = -I(V_{DD})$ driving $5\ \text{pF}$ capacitive loads per stage under BSIM4 nanoscale physical effects.
2. **Two-Conductor Biot-Savart Layout Model**: Evaluating the net superimposed magnetic flux density $B(t)$ generated by parallel outgoing ($V_{DD}$) and return ($GND$) power rails, and computing its time rate of change $\frac{dB(t)}{dt}$ as the physical signal captured by inductive near-field magnetic probes (Faraday's Law).
3. **High-Frequency Spectral & Time-Frequency Analysis**: Computing the discrete Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) and Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) spectrogram of $\frac{dB(t)}{dt}$, revealing transient emissions centered at **$21.5\ \text{MHz}$** with an effective bandwidth of $\approx 120\ \text{MHz}$.

### 2.2 State Transition Schedule and Directional Asymmetry
The 4-bit bus executes five transitions across the $6.0\ \mu\text{s}$ transient window:

| Time Interval | Input Bus ($B_3 B_2 B_1 B_0$) | Output Bus ($Y_3 Y_2 Y_1 Y_0$) | $HD$ | Active Transitions | Supply Current Surge ($I_{DD}$) | Physical Conduction Mechanism |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **$0.0\text{--}1.0\ \mu\text{s}$** | $0000_2$ | $1111_2$ | $0$ | Quiescent initial state | $\approx 0.00\ \text{mA}$ | Static DC state; subthreshold leakage only. |
| **$1.0\text{--}2.0\ \mu\text{s}$** | $0101_2$ | $1010_2$ | $2$ | $Y_0 \downarrow$, $Y_2 \downarrow$ (2 bits falling) | $\approx 0.06\ \text{mA}$ (short-circuit spike) | Load capacitors discharge through NMOS to GND. No charge pulled from $V_{DD}$. |
| **$2.0\text{--}3.0\ \mu\text{s}$** | $0111_2$ | $1000_2$ | $1$ | $Y_1 \downarrow$ (1 bit falling) | $\approx 0.03\ \text{mA}$ (short-circuit spike) | Single load capacitor discharges to GND. Negligible $V_{DD}$ rail current. |
| **$3.0\text{--}4.0\ \mu\text{s}$** | $0011_2$ | $1100_2$ | $1$ | $Y_2 \uparrow$ (1 bit rising) | **$\approx 1.33\ \text{mA}$** | PMOS pulls $C_2$ ($5\ \text{pF}$) from $0\ \text{V} \to 5\ \text{V}$, drawing $Q = C \Delta V$ from $V_{DD}$. |
| **$4.0\text{--}5.0\ \mu\text{s}$** | $1111_2$ | $0000_2$ | $2$ | $Y_2 \downarrow$, $Y_3 \downarrow$ (2 bits falling) | $\approx 0.06\ \text{mA}$ (short-circuit spike) | Discharging into GND return rail. |
| **$5.0\text{--}6.0\ \mu\text{s}$** | $0000_2$ | $1111_2$ | $4$ | $Y_0..Y_3 \uparrow$ (4 bits rising) | **$\approx 5.32\ \text{mA}$** | Massive parallel charging of all 4 load capacitors ($20\ \text{pF}$ total) through PMOS from $V_{DD}$. |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Directional Switching Asymmetry**: In CMOS circuits, current is drawn from the positive power supply ($V_{DD}$) primarily when outputs transition **LOW-to-HIGH** (charging load capacitors via PMOS). When outputs transition **HIGH-to-LOW**, stored electrostatic energy is discharged locally through NMOS channels directly into the ground plane. Therefore, $I_{DD}(t)$ strictly tracks the **Hamming Weight of 0-to-1 output bit flips**, demonstrating fundamental supply-rail directional asymmetry.

## 3. Two-Conductor Biot-Savart Layout Model

### 3.1 Geometric & Physical Formulation
The circuit layout is modeled as two parallel straight conductors embedded on a printed circuit board / integrated circuit metallization stack:
- **Conductor 1 ($V_{DD}$ rail)**: Distance $r_1 = 100\ \mu\text{m}$, carrying transient supply current $+I_{DD}(t)$.
- **Conductor 2 ($GND$ return rail)**: Distance $r_2 = r_1 + d = 120\ \mu\text{m}$ ($d = 20\ \mu\text{m}$ separation), carrying return current $-I_{DD}(t)$.

Applying the Biot-Savart Law for long straight conductors:
$$B_1(t) = \frac{\mu_0 \mu_r}{2\pi r_1} I_{DD}(t), \quad B_2(t) = -\frac{\mu_0 \mu_r}{2\pi r_2} I_{DD}(t)$$

The net superimposed magnetic flux density at the sensor observation point is:
$$B(t) = B_1(t) + B_2(t) = \frac{\mu_0 \mu_r}{2\pi} \left(\frac{1}{r_1} - \frac{1}{r_2}\right) I_{DD}(t)$$

Where:
- $\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7}\ \text{H/m}$ (vacuum permeability)
- $\mu_r = 1.0$ (dielectric relative permeability)
- Geometric scalar coupling factor:
  $$G = \frac{\mu}{2\pi} \left(\frac{1}{100 \times 10^{-6}} - \frac{1}{120 \times 10^{-6}}\right) \approx 3.333 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{T/A}$$

At peak supply current ($I_{DD,\max} = 5.3187\ \text{mA}$ at $t = 5.0\ \mu\text{s}$):
$$B_{\max} = 3.333 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{T/A} \times 5.3187 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{A} \approx 1.730\ \mu\text{T} \quad (1,729.72\ \text{nT})$$

## 4. Peak Rate of Change ($\frac{dB}{dt}$) and its Fundamental Relation to the FFT

### 4.1 Electromagnetic Induction & Sensor Voltage
Inductive near-field magnetic probes (such as loop probes or integrated pick-up coils) do not respond directly to static magnetic flux $B(t)$. According to **Faraday's Law of Induction**, the open-circuit voltage $\mathcal{E}(t)$ induced in a search coil of effective area $A$ and turns $N$ is:
$$\mathcal{E}(t) = -N \frac{d\Phi_B}{dt} = -N A \frac{dB(t)}{dt}$$
Hence, the time rate of change $\frac{dB(t)}{dt}$ represents the physical electromagnetic emission captured in side-channel analysis.

### 4.2 Mathematical Relationship: Derivative Property of Fourier Transform
By the differentiation property of the Fourier Transform:
$$\mathcal{F}\left\{\frac{dB(t)}{dt}\right\}(\omega) = j\omega \mathcal{F}\{B(t)\}(\omega) = j 2\pi f \mathcal{F}\{B(t)\}(f)$$
Taking the magnitude:
$$\left|\mathcal{F}\left\{\frac{dB(t)}{dt}\right\}(f)\right| = 2\pi f \cdot |\mathcal{F}\{B(t)\}(f)| = 2\pi f \cdot G \cdot \left|\mathcal{F}\{I_{DD}(t)\}(f)\right|$$

### 4.3 Why the Spectral Peak Appears at $21.5\ \text{MHz}$
1. **Linear High-Pass Weighting ($+20\ \text{dB/decade}$)**: The term $2\pi f$ acts as an ideal differentiator, attenuating low-frequency and DC components while heavily amplifying high-frequency transient transitions.
2. **BSIM4 Unified Slew Dynamics**: Under BSIM4, realistic channel resistance, continuous gate oxide capacitance (`TOXE = 10 nm`), and enhanced drive capability yield faster transition edges (rise times of $\approx 2.5\text{--}4\ \text{ns}$), resulting in a wider current pulse spectrum than BSIM3v3.3.
3. **Resonance Peak Equilibrium**: The multiplication of $2\pi f$ by the BSIM4 transient spectrum peaks where the $+20\ \text{dB/decade}$ derivative slope balances the spectral roll-off of the BSIM4 pulse, shifting the dominant emission frequency up to **$21.5\ \text{MHz}$**.
4. **Quantitative Rate of Change**: The maximum instantaneous derivative is:
   $$\left|\frac{dB}{dt}\right|_{\max} \approx 2.85\ \text{kT/s} \quad (2,849.6\ \text{T/s})$$

## 5. Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) Spectrogram Dynamics

The STFT decomposes $\frac{dB(t)}{dt}$ into simultaneous time-frequency energy distributions using a Hann window ($N_{\text{perseg}} = 1024$ samples $= 102.4\ \text{ns}$, $93.8\%$ overlap):
- **$t = 5.0\ \mu\text{s}$ Transition ($HD = 4$, 4 Bits Rising)**: Generates a concentrated spectral burst peaking at $21.5\ \text{MHz}$ and extending across $0\text{--}120\ \text{MHz}$.
- **$t = 3.0\ \mu\text{s}$ Transition ($HD = 1$, 1 Bit Rising)**: Generates a clear secondary spectral burst approximately $12\ \text{dB}$ lower in magnitude, proportional to single-capacitor charging.
- **Discharge Intervals ($t = 1.0\ \mu\text{s}, 2.0\ \mu\text{s}, 4.0\ \mu\text{s}$)**: Exhibit minimal high-frequency radiation on the supply loop due to ground-localized charge evacuation.
